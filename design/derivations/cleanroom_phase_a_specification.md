## Physical Problem

An artificial satellite orbits an oblate Earth. The gravitational potential includes
the second zonal harmonic $J_2$ (oblateness). The satellite also experiences
atmospheric drag proportional to the velocity squared.

## Variables and Notation

### Delaunay canonical variables
- $L = \sqrt{\mu a}$, conjugate to $l$ (mean anomaly)
- $G = L\eta = L\sqrt{1-e^2}$, conjugate to $g$ (argument of perigee)
- $H = G\cos I$, conjugate to $h$ (longitude of ascending node)

where $a$ = semimajor axis, $e$ = eccentricity, $I$ = inclination, $\mu$ = gravitational parameter.

### Shorthand
- $\eta = \sqrt{1-e^2} = G/L$
- $\theta = \cos I = H/G$
- $n = \mu^2/L^3$ (mean motion)
- $r$ = radius, $f$ = true anomaly, $u$ = eccentric anomaly
- $p = a\eta^2 = a(1-e^2)$ (semi-latus rectum)

### Kepler orbit relations
- $r = a(1 - e\cos u)$
- $r\cos f = a(\cos u - e)$
- $r\sin f = a\eta\sin u$
- Kepler's equation: $l = u - e\sin u$
- Vis-viva: $V^2 = \mu(2/r - 1/a)$
- $\eta a^2 dl = r^2 df$ (differential relation between mean and true anomaly)

### Earth model constants
- $k_2 = \frac{1}{2}J_2 R_\oplus^2$ (second harmonic coefficient, with Earth radius $R_\oplus$)

## The Hamiltonian

The gravitational Hamiltonian in Delaunay variables is:

$$\mathcal{H} = \mathcal{H}_0 + k_2 \mathcal{H}_1$$

where:
$$\mathcal{H}_0 = -\frac{\mu^2}{2L^2}$$

$$\mathcal{H}_1 = -\frac{\mu^4}{L^3} \frac{1}{r^3} \left[\left(-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{3}{2}\theta^2\right) + \left(\frac{3}{2}-\frac{3}{2}\theta^2\right)\cos(2g+2f)\right]$$

Note: $-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{3}{2}\theta^2 = 1-\frac{3}{2}\sin^2 I$ is the same quantity.

## The drag model

The drag acceleration on each Cartesian velocity component is:
$$X_j = -AV\exp(-\alpha r)\xi_j$$

where $A$ is the drag coefficient times a density scale, $\alpha$ is the atmospheric
density scale height parameter, $V$ is the orbital velocity, and $\xi_j = \dot{x}_j$.

In Delaunay variables, the drag perturbation is expressed through:
$$P_j = -AV\exp(-\alpha r)p_j, \quad Q_j = -AV\exp(-\alpha r)q_j$$

where $P_j$ and $Q_j$ are the non-Hamiltonian perturbation terms in the canonical
equations of motion:
$$\frac{dL_j}{dt} = \frac{\partial\mathcal{H}}{\partial l_j} + P_j, \quad \frac{dl_j}{dt} = -\frac{\partial\mathcal{H}}{\partial L_j} - Q_j$$

The $p_j$ and $q_j$ are:
$$p_1 = L\left[\frac{2a}{r}-1\right], \quad q_1 = 2e\sin u + \frac{2\eta}{e}\sin f$$
$$p_2 = G, \quad q_2 = -\frac{2}{e}\sin f$$
$$p_3 = H, \quad q_3 = 0$$

## The canonical transformation

A canonical transformation $(L_j, l_j) \to (L_j'', l_j'')$ is constructed so that
the transformed Hamiltonian $F^{**}$ depends only on the momenta $(L_1'', L_2'', L_3'')$
and not on the angles. This is the Brouwer transformation.

The transformation is generated by a determining function $S_1 + S_1^*$
(first-order in $k_2$), where:

### Construction of $S_1$ (the short-period generating function)

$S_1$ is obtained by integrating the short-period part of the perturbation
over the mean anomaly:

$$S_1 = \frac{1}{n}\int\left(\mathcal{H}_1 - \langle\mathcal{H}_1\rangle_l\right) dl$$

where $\langle\mathcal{H}_1\rangle_l$ is the average of $\mathcal{H}_1$ over one orbit (over $l$).

Using the relation $\eta a^2 dl = r^2 df$, this integral can be evaluated in
terms of the true anomaly $f$.

### Construction of $S_1^*$ (the long-period generating function)

$S_1^*$ is an integration constant chosen to eliminate long-period terms
(terms depending on $g$ but not $l$) at second order. It has the form:
$$S_1^* = C_1(L, G, H) \cdot \sin 2g$$
where $C_1$ is determined by the requirement that the second-order
averaged Hamiltonian has no $g$-dependent terms.

## The operator D

Define the operator:
$$D = -\sum_j \xi_j'' \frac{\partial}{\partial \xi_j''}$$

where $\xi_j'' = \dot{x}_j''$ are the Cartesian velocity components expressed
in the doubly-primed variables. $D$ counts the "velocity homogeneity degree."

Key properties:
- $DL_j'' = -(p_j)''$ (from the definition of $p_j$)
- $Dl_j'' = +(q_j)''$
- $DI'' = 0$ (inclination is independent of velocity)
- $Dr'' = 0$ (radius is position-only)

The oblateness corrections to the drag perturbation are:
$$\delta p_j = D\left(\frac{\partial(S_1 + S_1^*)}{\partial l_j''}\right), \quad \delta q_j = D\left(\frac{\partial(S_1 + S_1^*)}{\partial L_j''}\right)$$

## TASK: Derive the following quantities

### Task 1: Construct $S_1$ explicitly

Starting from $\mathcal{H}_1$, compute $\langle\mathcal{H}_1\rangle_l$, then integrate
$\mathcal{H}_1 - \langle\mathcal{H}_1\rangle_l$ over $l$ to obtain $S_1$.

Express the result in terms of $a, e, \eta, \theta, f, g, l$ (and $r$ where convenient).

### Task 2: Construct $S_1^*$

Determine $S_1^*$ by requiring that long-period terms vanish in the
second-order averaged Hamiltonian. Express the result.

### Task 3: Compute $\delta p_j$ and $\delta q_j$ for $j = 1, 2, 3$

Apply the operator $D$ to $\partial(S_1 + S_1^*)/\partial l_j''$ and $\partial(S_1 + S_1^*)/\partial L_j''$
respectively. Express the results in closed form (in terms of $a/r$, $f$, $g$, $e$, $\theta$, $\eta$).

Show every intermediate step:
- The partial derivatives $\partial S_1/\partial l_j$, $\partial S_1/\partial L_j$
- The action of $D$ on each term
- The final collected result

### Task 4: Compute the drag-free periodic corrections $\delta a$, $\delta e$, $\delta I$, $\delta l$

These are obtained from the Poisson brackets of the orbital elements with $S_1 + S_1^*$:
- $\delta a = \{a, S_1 + S_1^*\} = \frac{2}{na}\frac{\partial(S_1+S_1^*)}{\partial l}$
- $\delta e$ involves $\partial(S_1+S_1^*)/\partial l$ and $\partial(S_1+S_1^*)/\partial g$
- $\delta I$ involves $\partial(S_1+S_1^*)/\partial g$ and $\partial(S_1+S_1^*)/\partial h$
- $\delta l$ involves $\partial(S_1+S_1^*)/\partial L$ plus corrections

Express results in closed form.

For each derived quantity, verify numerically:

Use test cases: $e = 0.01, 0.1$; $I = 30°, 60°, 85°$; $g = 0°, 45°, 90°$.
