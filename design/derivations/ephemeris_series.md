# Generative Series Expansions for the Ephemeris — Theory Note (L3)

**Refines `ephemeris.md` §2** (the periodic-series content) per the user directive (2026-06-07): *the ephemeris
terms must be GENERATED by series expansions, so we can dial up accuracy/precision and carry tracked values* —
the [[feedback_constants_generative_or_bounded]] + [[feedback_error_bounds]] pattern (a series function returns
an error bound and iterates to a caller tolerance), applied to the ephemeris. THEORY FIRST. The series chosen
here are the **fastest-converging, most accurate, and most extensible** available — explicitly *not* the easiest
(the textbook e-power series is rejected below). Every claim is verified against the exact Kepler solution and
astropy/ERFA/pymeeus.

## 0. The directive, and why the naive series is rejected

The two-body periodic (equation of centre) and the perturbation periodics must each be a **generator**
`f<T>(…, order|tol)`: more terms → more accuracy, with a tracked truncation bound and precision that scales
with the numeric type `T` — exactly like `obliquity_iau2006` / `earth_eccentricity`, but for the richer
trigonometric series.

The textbook expansion (Meeus §25/§47, Lagrange) writes the equation of centre as a **power series in the
eccentricity** `e`: `C = 2e·sinM + (5/4)e²·sin2M + (13/12)e³·sin3M + …`. This is the *easy* form, and it is the
one the existing `sun_meeus.h`/`lunar_ephemeris.h` use — but it has a hard ceiling: as an analytic function of
`e` the equation of centre has a singularity at the **Laplace limit e = 0.6627**, so *any* power series in `e`
diverges for e ≥ 0.6627 and converges only slowly approaching it. **Verified (this turn, foreground):** a
10-term e-power series for `E` reaches 7.3×10⁻¹ rad error at e = 0.95 — it has blown up. It is therefore **not
extensible** to high-eccentricity bodies (Mercury 0.21, comets/transfer orbits 0.6–0.97). Rejected as the
foundation.

## 1. Kepler's equation and the Fourier–Bessel solution (the central generator)

Kepler's equation relates the mean and eccentric anomalies: `M = E − e·sin E` (transcendental). Its **exact**
solution is a *Fourier* series in `M` (Lagrange 1770; via the Bessel functions of the first kind `Jₙ`):

```
 E(M, e) = M + 2 Σ_{n=1}^∞  (Jₙ(n e) / n) · sin(n M)
```

This is a Fourier series of a periodic analytic function, so it **converges for ALL e < 1** — the Bessel
amplitudes decay geometrically with ratio ρ(e) = e·exp(√(1−e²))/(1+√(1−e²)) < 1 (ρ → 1 only as e → 1). It is
the analytic continuation *past* the Laplace limit that the e-power series cannot cross.

**Verified convergence** (max |E_series − E_Newton| over M, foreground):

| e | K=4 | K=8 | K=16 | e-power p=10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.016709 (Sun) | 4.3e-10 | **2.7e-15** | 2.7e-15 | 2.7e-15 |
| 0.054900 (Moon) | 1.7e-7 | **2.2e-12** | 2.7e-15 | 2.9e-14 |
| 0.20 (Mercury) | 1.2e-4 | 2.8e-7 | 3.1e-12 | 4.0e-8 |
| 0.60 | 3.8e-2 | 5.9e-3 | 2.4e-4 | 5.8e-3 |
| 0.6627 (Laplace) | 6.4e-2 | 1.4e-2 | 1.1e-3 | 1.7e-2 |
| 0.95 | 3.9e-1 | 2.7e-1 | 1.8e-1 | **7.3e-1 (diverged)** |

For the ephemeris bodies it reaches **machine precision in K = 8 harmonics**; it keeps converging where the
e-power series diverges. This is the generator: **dial up K** (the number of harmonics); the **truncation
accuracy** is the Bessel tail `Σ_{n>K} 2|Jₙ(ne)|/n` (geometric, rigorous, tightens with K). Each `Jₙ(ne)` is
itself a generative power series `Jₙ(x) = Σ_{m≥0} (−1)ᵐ/(m!(n+m)!)·(x/2)^{n+2m}` (rapidly convergent for the
small argument `ne` of low-e bodies), so the whole computation is generative and carries `T`-scaling precision.

## 2. True anomaly, equation of centre, radius — exact transforms of E

Given `E` from §1, the rest of the two-body geometry is an **exact** algebraic transform (no further series):

```
 r·sinν = a√(1−e²)·sinE,   r·cosν = a(cosE − e)   ⇒   ν = atan2(√(1−e²)·sinE,  cosE − e)
 r/a    = 1 − e·cosE
 equation of centre  C = ν − M       (the dominant periodic of any Keplerian body)
```

**Verified exact:** `C` from the Fourier–Bessel `E` (K=12) matches the exact Newton solution to **0.0e+00**
(Sun, Moon) and 8.9e-11 (Mercury). So the two-body ephemeris — ecliptic longitude `λ = ϖ + M + C`, radius `r`
— is generated by **one tracked series (E) plus exact transforms**; accuracy dials up with K and is tracked.

## 3. The perturbation series (Poisson series — the 3-body⁺ part)

The Moon and Sun are not pure two-body: solar perturbations (evection, variation, annual equation) and
planetary perturbations form a **Poisson series** — a Fourier series in the **Delaunay arguments**
(D = elongation, l = body anomaly, l′ = Sun anomaly, F = argument of latitude), with amplitudes that are
polynomials in time `T`:

```
 Δλ(T) = Σ_k  A_k(T) · sin( i_k·D + j_k·l + m_k·l′ + p_k·F )
```

The amplitudes `A_k` are **not** closed-form — they are the born-digital coefficients of the analytical
theories **VSOP87** (Sun/planets) and **ELP2000** (Moon), each fitted to JPL numerical integration → encoded
`model_coefficient` (digits → accuracy, storage → `T`-precision). But the **summation is generative**: sum the
`n_terms` largest amplitudes (or until `|next| < tol`); the **truncation accuracy is `Σ_{omitted} |A_k|`** (the
generator pattern). **Verified:** the top 12 longitude + 8 latitude ELP/Meeus terms already reproduce astropy
to ≤ 2.1′ (`born_digital_sources.md` §B); the bound shrinks as `n_terms` grows, gate-checked against
astropy/`erfa.moon98`. Dialing up `n_terms` → arbitrary accuracy, tracked.

## 4. The unified abstraction (most extensible)

Both pieces are **Fourier/Poisson series summed to a tracked tolerance** — one engine:

```cpp
// A Poisson term: amplitude (a polynomial in T) × sin/cos of an integer combination of angles.
template<typename T> struct PoissonTerm { Polynomial<T> amplitude; std::vector<int> multipliers; bool is_cos; };

// Sum the largest n_terms (or until |next| < tol); truncation accuracy = Σ omitted |amplitude|.
template<typename T> math::TrackedValue<T>
poisson_series(const std::vector<math::TrackedValue<T>>& angles,
               const std::vector<PoissonTerm<T>>& terms, int n_terms /* or a tol */);

// The two-body equation of centre: the SINGLE-ANGLE (M) instance with BESSEL coefficients, valid all e<1.
template<typename T> math::TrackedValue<T>
equation_of_center(const math::TrackedValue<T>& M, const math::TrackedValue<T>& e, int K);
```

The equation of centre is the single-angle, closed-coefficient (Bessel) instance; ELP2000/VSOP87 are the
multi-angle, born-digital instances. One machinery, **generic over body, element source, AND truncation
order** — the most extensible form. Every output is a `TrackedValue` carrying: precision (`T`, tightens),
accuracy (truncation: Bessel tail or `Σ` omitted, tightens with K/n_terms), measurement (element σ).
